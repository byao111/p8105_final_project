## The tentative project title:

Beyond the Grade: Exploring Food Safety Disparities Across New York City
Restaurants

## The motivation for this project

Food safety inspections are essential for protecting public health and
reducing foodborne illness risks. However, inspection outcomes may vary
across geographic locations and restaurant characteristics in a diverse
city like New York City. Using publicly available data from the NYC
Department of Health and Mental Hygiene (DOHMH), this project aims to
examine differences in inspection scores, grades, and violation patterns
across boroughs and cuisine types, providing insights into potential
disparities in restaurant food safety.

## The intended final products

There will be three main project products. First, we will write a
reproducible research report using R Markdown, which will document the
process of data analysis, statistical methods and main findings. This
report will focus on describing the differences and relationships in
food safety inspection results, violation types, and inspection grades
among restaurants across New York City’s five boroughs. Second, we will
build a project website to present the research background, questions,
main findings and data visualizations. This website will also provide
the complete research report and the link to our GitHub repository.
Finally, we will create a 2-minutes video to introduce the research
motivation, key research problems and main findings. This video will be
embedded in the project website.

## The anticipated data sources

We will use the NYC Department of Health and Mental Hygiene (DOHMH)
Restaurant Inspection Results dataset, available through NYC Open Data:
<https://data.cityofnewyork.us/d/43nn-pn8j>.

The dataset contains restaurant identifiers, boroughs, cuisine
classifications, inspection dates, inspection types, violation codes,
critical violation indicators, and inspection scores.

## The planned analyses / visualizations / coding challenges

We will use R and tidyverse to clean the data and examine differences in
inspection scores and violations across boroughs and cuisine types.
Boxplots, bar charts, and interactive maps will be used to visualize key
patterns, while statistical tests and regression models will explore
associations. Main challenges include handling missing values, duplicate
records, and repeated inspections to ensure reliable results.
